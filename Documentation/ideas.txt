A world model is an internal, mathematical representation of reality that an AI agent uses to "understand" and "predict" the outcomes of its actions.

Think of it as a mental simulator. Instead of a robot having to physically bang into a wall 1,000 times to learn it's an obstacle, a world model allows the robot to "dream" or simulate that collision internally. This makes training significantly faster and safer.
1. The Core Components

In the classic architecture popularized by David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber, a world model is typically composed of three distinct parts:

    The Vision Model (V): Usually a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) or a Vision Transformer. It takes high-dimensional raw data (like camera pixels) and compresses it into a small, manageable latent vector (z).

    The Memory Model (M): Often a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) or a Transformer. It predicts how the world will change over time. It takes the current latent state and an action to predict what the next state will look like.

    The Controller (C): A simple, lightweight model that decides which action to take based solely on the "mental" representation provided by V and M.
